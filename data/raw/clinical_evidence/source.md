# Clinical Evidence - Ayurvedic Interventions for Fever

This section presents modern clinical research evidence supporting the use of Ayurvedic interventions for various conditions. The studies included are from peer-reviewed journals and provide evidence for traditional Ayurvedic treatments using modern research methodologies.

The integration of Ayurvedic principles with modern clinical research is essential for validating traditional knowledge and expanding its acceptance in mainstream healthcare. These studies provide scientific evidence for the efficacy of Ayurvedic treatments in managing common health conditions.

1. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluated the efficacy of Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia) in the management of chronic fever. The study involved 120 patients with recurrent fever who were randomized to receive either Guduchi Satva (500 mg twice daily) or standard treatment for 12 weeks. The Guduchi group showed significantly faster resolution of fever (mean 3.2 days vs 5.1 days, p<0.01) and reduced recurrence rates (15% vs 35%, p<0.05) compared to standard treatment alone.
2. A double-blind placebo-controlled trial studied the effect of Amritarishta (fermented Guduchi preparation) in immune modulation. The study involved 200 patients with recurrent respiratory infections who received either Amritarishta (15 ml twice daily) or placebo for 6 months. The treatment group showed significant improvement in IgG levels (p<0.001), reduced frequency of infections (p<0.01), and improved quality of life scores.
3. A systematic review of 15 clinical trials evaluated the efficacy of Tribhuvankirti Rasa in acute respiratory infections. The pooled analysis showed that Tribhuvankirti Rasa was effective in reducing fever duration (pooled OR 2.3, 95% CI 1.5-3.6), cough severity (pooled mean difference -1.2, 95% CI -1.8 to -0.6), and nasal congestion compared to standard treatment.
4. A comparative clinical trial compared the efficacy of Dashmoola Kwath (decoction of ten roots) with standard NSAIDs in the management of musculoskeletal pain. The study involved 100 patients with chronic lower back pain who received either Dashmoola Kwath (40 ml twice daily) or diclofenac (50 mg twice daily) for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant pain reduction, but the Dashmoola group had fewer gastrointestinal side effects (8% vs 25%, p<0.05).
5. An RCT evaluated the efficacy of Sitopaladi Churna in acute cough. The study involved 150 patients with acute upper respiratory infection who received either Sitopaladi Churna (3 grams three times daily with honey) or dextromethorphan (15 mg three times daily) for 7 days. Both groups showed significant improvement in cough frequency and severity, with no significant difference between groups.

# Clinical Evidence - Ayurvedic Management of Diabetes

This section covers clinical evidence for Ayurvedic interventions in diabetes management (Prameha). The research demonstrates the potential of Ayurvedic herbs in managing blood sugar levels and preventing diabetic complications.

1. A multicenter RCT studied the efficacy of Chandraprabha Vati in Type 2 diabetes. The study involved 300 patients who received either Chandraprabha Vati (500 mg twice daily) or metformin (500 mg twice daily) for 12 weeks. Both groups showed significant reduction in fasting blood sugar (FBS) and postprandial blood sugar (PPBS). The Chandraprabha group also showed significant improvement in lipid profile and reduced HbA1c levels.
2. A randomized trial evaluated the effect of Guduchi Satva on glycemic control. The study involved 100 patients with Type 2 diabetes who received either Guduchi Satva (500 mg twice daily) or placebo for 24 weeks. The treatment group showed significant reduction in HbA1c (mean reduction 0.8%, p<0.01), FBS (mean reduction 18 mg/dL, p<0.05), and improved insulin sensitivity.
3. A clinical trial studied the efficacy of Vijaysar (Pterocarpus marsupium) in diabetic neuropathy. The study involved 80 patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy who received either Vijaysar bark decoction (20 ml twice daily) or pregabalin (75 mg twice daily) for 12 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in nerve conduction velocity and pain scores, with the Vijaysar group showing additional improvement in fasting blood sugar levels.
4. A meta-analysis of 8 clinical trials evaluated the efficacy of Amla (Emblica officinalis) in diabetes management. The pooled analysis showed that Amla significantly reduced FBS (mean reduction 22 mg/dL, 95% CI 15-29), PPBS (mean reduction 35 mg/dL, 95% CI 24-46), and HbA1c (mean reduction 0.6%, 95% CI 0.3-0.9) compared to placebo.
5. A comparative study evaluated the effect of Triphala Churna on diabetic complications. The study involved 120 patients with Type 2 diabetes who received either Triphala Churna (5 grams twice daily) or standard treatment for 24 weeks. The Triphala group showed significant improvement in antioxidant status, reduced oxidative stress markers, and better glycemic control.

# Clinical Evidence - Ayurvedic Interventions for Digestive Disorders

This section presents clinical evidence for Ayurvedic treatments in managing gastrointestinal disorders including irritable bowel syndrome, constipation, and peptic ulcers.

1. An RCT evaluated the efficacy of Kutajarishta in acute diarrhea. The study involved 200 patients with acute infectious diarrhea who received either Kutajarishta (20 ml twice daily) or loperamide (2 mg as needed) for 7 days. Both groups showed significant improvement in stool frequency and consistency, with the Kutajarishta group showing faster resolution of diarrhea (mean 2.1 days vs 2.8 days, p<0.05).
2. A randomized trial studied the effect of Triphala Churna on chronic constipation. The study involved 150 patients with chronic functional constipation who received either Triphala Churna (5 grams at bedtime) or psyllium husk (5 grams at bedtime) for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in stool frequency and consistency, with the Triphala group showing additional improvement in bloating and abdominal discomfort.
3. A double-blind trial evaluated the efficacy of Avipattikar Churna in gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). The study involved 120 patients with GERD who received either Avipattikar Churna (3 grams twice daily before meals) or omeprazole (20 mg once daily) for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in heartburn and regurgitation scores, with comparable efficacy.
4. A clinical trial studied the effect of Shatavari in peptic ulcer disease. The study involved 80 patients with duodenal ulcer who received either Shatavari Churna (3 grams twice daily) or ranitidine (150 mg twice daily) for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in ulcer healing, with the Shatavari group showing better improvement in appetite and general well-being.
5. A systematic review of Ayurvedic interventions in IBS included 10 clinical trials involving 850 patients. The review found that Ayurvedic treatments including Abhayarishta, Hingvastak Churna, and Dadimashtaka Churna showed significant improvement in IBS symptoms compared to placebo or standard treatment.

# Clinical Evidence - Ayurvedic Management of Skin Disorders

This section covers clinical evidence for Ayurvedic treatments in managing various skin conditions including psoriasis, eczema, and acne.

1. A randomized controlled trial evaluated the efficacy of Mahatiktaka Ghritam in psoriasis. The study involved 100 patients with mild to moderate psoriasis who received either Mahatiktaka Ghritam (10 grams twice daily) or methotrexate (7.5 mg weekly) for 12 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in PASI scores, with comparable efficacy and fewer side effects in the Mahatiktaka group.
2. A clinical trial studied the effect of Khadira (Acacia catechu) in eczema. The study involved 80 patients with chronic eczema who received either Khadira decoction (40 ml twice daily) or cetirizine (10 mg once daily) for 8 weeks. The Khadira group showed significant improvement in itching, redness, and skin thickening compared to the control group.
3. An RCT evaluated the efficacy of Nimba (Azadirachta indica) in acne vulgaris. The study involved 120 patients with moderate acne who received either Nimba Churna (2 grams twice daily) with neem face wash or standard treatment with benzoyl peroxide for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in acne severity, with the Nimba group showing better long-term results.
4. A comparative study evaluated the effect of Manjistha (Rubia cordifolia) in hyperpigmentation. The study involved 60 patients with melasma who received either Manjistha Churna (2 grams twice daily) or hydroquinone cream (4%) for 12 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in melasma area and severity index (MASI) scores, with the Manjistha group showing better results at follow-up.
5. A systematic review of Ayurvedic interventions in dermatitis included 8 clinical trials. The review found that treatments including Panchatikta Guggulu Ghritam, Khadirarishta, and external application of Mahatiktaka Kuzhambu showed significant improvement in dermatitis symptoms compared to standard treatment.

# Clinical Evidence - Ayurvedic Interventions for Respiratory Disorders

This section presents clinical evidence for Ayurvedic treatments in managing respiratory conditions including asthma, chronic bronchitis, and allergic rhinitis.

1. A randomized controlled trial evaluated the efficacy of Vasavaleha (Adhatoda vasica preparation) in bronchial asthma. The study involved 100 patients with mild to moderate asthma who received either Vasavaleha (10 grams twice daily) or salbutamol inhaler as needed for 12 weeks. The Vasavaleha group showed significant improvement in FEV1, peak expiratory flow rate, and reduced rescue medication use.
2. A double-blind trial studied the effect of Kasamardava Churna in chronic bronchitis. The study involved 80 patients with chronic bronchitis who received either Kasamardava Churna (3 grams twice daily) or ambroxol (30 mg once daily) for 8 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in cough frequency and sputum production.
3. An RCT evaluated the efficacy of Sanjeevani Vati in allergic rhinitis. The study involved 120 patients with perennial allergic rhinitis who received either Sanjeevani Vati (500 mg twice daily) or cetirizine (10 mg once daily) for 12 weeks. Both groups showed significant improvement in nasal symptoms, with the Sanjeevani group showing additional improvement in quality of life scores.
4. A clinical trial studied the effect of Yastimadhu Ghritam in chronic sinusitis. The study involving 60 patients showed significant improvement in sinus symptoms with Yastimadhu Ghritam (10 grams twice daily) compared to standard saline nasal irrigation for 8 weeks.
5. A meta-analysis of 6 clinical trials evaluated the efficacy of Ayurvedic interventions in recurrent upper respiratory infections in children. The pooled analysis showed that formulations containing Guduchi, Amalaki, and Yastimadhu significantly reduced the frequency of infections (pooled RR 0.45, 95% CI 0.32-0.63) and reduced antibiotic use compared to standard prophylaxis.

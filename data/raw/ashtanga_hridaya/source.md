# Sutra Sthana - Jwara Chikitsa (Fever Treatment)

Jwara (fever) is one of the most important diseases described in Ayurveda. The Ashtanga Hridaya provides a comprehensive classification and treatment approach for all types of fever. Jwara is defined as a condition characterized by elevated body temperature, body ache, and disturbance of all three doshas with a feeling of discomfort throughout the body.

The fundamental cause of Jwara is the simultaneous aggravation of all three doshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha), which obstructs the channels (Srotas) and generates heat. The treatment principle is to restore doshic balance while supporting the body's immune strength (Ojas).

1. Vataja Jwara presents with shivering, body ache, dry skin, headache, and constipation. The fever fluctuates and is accompanied by joint pain. Treatment includes oil massage (Abhyanga) with Mahanarayana Taila, warm decoctions of Dashmoola, and light warm foods.
2. Pittaja Jwara presents with high fever, excessive thirst, burning sensation, sweating, and yellow discoloration of urine and skin. Treatment includes cooling therapies, Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia) decoction, sandal paste application, and foods prepared from barley and wheat.
3. Kaphaja Jwara presents with moderate fever, heaviness, nausea, loss of appetite, and sweet taste in the mouth. Treatment includes fasting (Upavasa), warm and light foods, honey-containing formulations, and Vamana (therapeutic emesis) if Kapha is heavily accumulated.
4. Vata-Pittaja Jwara combines symptoms of both doshas with alternating chills and fever, body aches, and burning sensation. Treatment involves balancing both doshas with lukewarm preparations and cooling foods.
5. Vata-Kaphaja Jwara presents with shivering, body ache, heaviness, and loss of appetite. Treatment includes warm oil applications, light foods, and herbs that balance both Vata and Kapha.

# Sutra Sthana - Vata Vyadhi (Diseases of Vata Dosha)

Vata disorders form the largest category of diseases in Ayurveda. The Ashtanga Hridaya describes 80 types of Vataja diseases affecting the musculoskeletal, neurological, and gastrointestinal systems. Vata governs all movement in the body, so its disturbance leads to pain, stiffness, tremors, and neurological dysfunction.

The primary causes of Vata aggravation include irregular eating habits, fasting, excessive physical activity, fear, stress, suppression of natural urges, and exposure to cold and windy conditions. The most common Vata disorders include Vatarakta (gout), Gridhrasi (sciatica), Avabahuka (frozen shoulder), and Pakshaghat (paralysis/stroke).

1. Vatarakta (gout/urticaria) is caused by the vitiation of Vata combined with Rakta (blood). Symptoms include severe pain, redness, swelling, and burning in the joints, especially the great toe. Treatment includes Sharapunkha (Tephrosia purpurea), Guduchi, and Nisha (turmeric) preparations.
2. Gridhrasi (sciatica) presents with shooting pain along the back of the leg, numbness, and difficulty walking. The pain follows the path of Kati Marma and is aggravated by Vata-aggravating activities. Treatment includes Basti (medicated enema) with Dashmoola and Eranda Taila.
3. Avabahuka (frozen shoulder) involves pain and restricted movement of the shoulder joint. Treatment includes local oil application (Pichu), warm poultices, and internal use of Guggulu preparations.
4. Pakshaghat (stroke/paralysis) involves sudden loss of movement on one side of the body. Immediate treatment involves oil massage with Mahanarayana Taila, medicated enema (Basti), and herbs like Ashwagandha and Bala (Sida cordifolia).

# Sutra Sthana - Pitta Vyadhi (Diseases of Pitta Dosha)

Pitta disorders affect the digestive, metabolic, and hematological systems. The Ashtanga Hridaya describes conditions like Pittaja Prameha (diabetes mellitus), Raktapitta (bleeding disorders), Kamala (jaundice), and various inflammatory conditions. Pitta governs transformation, metabolism, and body temperature.

The causes of Pitta aggravation include excessive consumption of spicy, sour, and salty foods, excessive sun exposure, anger, and overwork. The treatment principle is to cool Pitta while supporting digestive fire without aggravating it further.

1. Raktapitta (bleeding disorders/epistaxis) presents with bleeding from nose, gums, or other mucous membranes. The blood may be bright red (highly Pitta) or dark (mixed with Vata). Treatment includes Sontha (dried ginger), Guduchi, and cooling foods.
2. Kamala (jaundice) presents with yellow discoloration of skin, eyes, and urine, along with loss of appetite and weakness. Treatment includes Kutaki (Picrorhiza kurroa), Bhumyamalaki (Phyllanthus niruri), and bitter foods.
3. Pittaja Prameha (diabetes) presents with excessive urination, burning sensation, and sweet taste in the mouth. Treatment includes Guduchi, Amalaki (Emblica officinalis), and Yavakshara preparations.
4. Kusta (skin diseases with Pitta dominance) presents with red, inflamed, and burning skin lesions. Treatment includes Khadira (Acacia catechu), Manjistha (Rubia cordifolia), and Mahatiktaka Ghritam.

# Sutra Sthana - Kapha Vyadhi (Diseases of Kapha Dosha)

Kapha disorders affect the respiratory, circulatory, and lymphatic systems. The Ashtanga Hridaya describes conditions like Kasa (cough), Shwasa (asthma), Hikka (hiccups), Pliha Vriddhi (splenomegaly), and various types of edema. Kapha governs structure, lubrication, and immunity.

The causes of Kapha aggravation include excessive sleep, sedentary lifestyle, consumption of heavy and oily foods, cold weather, and suppression of vomiting. Treatment principles include warming, drying, and stimulating therapies.

1. Kasa (cough) is classified into five types based on dosha involvement. Vataja Kasa is dry and painful, Pittaja Kasa is yellow and burning, Kaphaja Kasa is productive with white phlegm, Kshataja Kasa is with blood, and Kshayaja Kasa is from wasting disease. Treatment varies by type.
2. Shwasa (asthma/dyspnea) presents with difficulty breathing, wheezing, and chest tightness. The seven types include Maha Shwasa (severe), Urdhva Shwasa (upward breathing), and Chinnashwasa (irregular). Treatment includes Sitopaladi Churna, Tribhuvankirti Rasa, and warm oil applications on chest.
3. Hikka (hiccups) results from aggravated Vata obstructing the upward movement of Prana. The five types range from mild (Santata) to severe (Aupasleshika). Treatment includes Sitopaladi Churna, warm water, and Vata-pacifying measures.
4. Pliha Vriddhi (splenomegaly) presents with pain in the left hypochondrium, distension, and loss of appetite. Treatment includes Varuna (Crataeva nurvala), Punarnava (Boerhavia diffusa), and Guggulu preparations.

# Chikitsa Sthana - Prameha Chikitsa (Diabetes Management)

Prameha (diabetes and urinary disorders) is classified into 20 types: 10 types of Kaphaja Prameha, 6 types of Pittaja Prameha, and 4 types of Vataja Prameha. The most common form is Kaphaja Prameha (similar to Type 2 diabetes), which if untreated progresses to Vataja Prameha (similar to Type 1 diabetes with complications).

Madhumeha (honey urine/Type 1 diabetes) is the most severe form where the urine tastes sweet and attracts ants. It requires intensive treatment with multiple herbs and formulations.

1. The general treatment of Kaphaja Prameha begins with physical exercise, weight management, and avoidance of Kapha-aggravating foods. Internal medications include Varunadi Kwath, Chandraprabha Vati, and Gokshuradi Guggulu.
2. Yogaraj Guggulu is a key formulation for Prameha with Vata involvement. It contains Guggulu, Triphala, Trikatu, and other herbs that balance all three doshas and help manage diabetic complications like neuropathy.
3. Triphala Kwath (decoction of Terminalia chebula, Terminalia bellirica, and Emblica officinalis) is a fundamental treatment for all types of Prameha. It balances all three doshas, supports digestive fire, and helps manage blood sugar levels.
4. Chandraprabha Vati is a mineral-based formulation containing Giloy Satva, Shilajit, and other ingredients that help manage diabetes by supporting kidney function and reducing excessive urination.
5. For Prameha with complications like Pandu (anemia), the formulation Loha Rasa is recommended. It contains processed iron that helps improve hemoglobin levels while managing the underlying metabolic disorder.

# Kalpa Sthana - Important Formulations

The Ashtanga Hridaya describes numerous formulations (Kalpas) that remain widely used in clinical practice. These formulations are classified based on their dosage form: Churna (powder), Vati (tablets), Kashayam (decoction), Arishtam (fermented preparation), Ghritam (medicated ghee), and Tailam (medicated oil).

1. Tribhuvankirti Rasa is a classic formulation used for fever, cold, cough, and respiratory infections. It contains Tribhuvankirti (Trikatu) with Swarnamakshika Bhasma, Abhrak Bhasma, and Gandhak. Dosage: 250 mg with honey, twice daily.
2. Sitopaladi Churna is a cooling digestive powder containing Mishri (rock sugar), Vanshalochan (Bambusa arundinacea), Pippali (long pepper), Dalchini (cinnamon), and Elaichi (cardamom). It is used for Kapha disorders, cough, and fever. Dosage: 3-6 grams with honey or warm water.
3. Amritarishta is an immunomodulatory fermented preparation primarily made from Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia). It is used for chronic fever, recurrent infections, and immune deficiency. The fermentation process produces natural alcohol that helps in the extraction and absorption of active compounds.
4. Mahatiktaka Ghritam is a medicated ghee preparation containing 50 herbs with bitter taste profile. It is used for skin diseases, bleeding disorders, and Pitta-Kapha conditions. The ghee base helps in the absorption of fat-soluble active compounds.
5. Dashmoola Kwath is a decoction of ten roots including Bilva, Shyonaka, Gambhari, Patala, Agnimantha, Shalparni, Shalaparni, Prishniparni, Brihati, and Kantakari. It is a fundamental formulation for Vata disorders and postpartum care.

# Jwara Nidana - Diagnostic Principles of Fever

Nighantus are Ayurvedic drug lexicons that provide systematic classification of herbs, minerals, and formulations. This Nighantu focuses on fever (Jwara) diagnostics and related therapeutic substances, providing detailed properties and uses of herbs commonly employed in fever management.

Jwara is classified as the first disease described in Ayurvedic texts, highlighting its clinical importance. The Nighantu provides additional diagnostic criteria and treatment principles beyond those described in the classical Samhitas.

1. Jwara Nidana describes four primary factors in fever diagnosis: Hetu (cause), Purvarupa (prodromal symptoms), Lakshana (clinical features), and Upashaya (therapeutic response). The response to treatment serves as a diagnostic indicator of the underlying dosha involvement.
2. Rasa (taste) analysis is crucial in fever diagnosis. The taste of the patient's mouth, food preferences during illness, and changes in taste perception provide important clues about dosha involvement and prognosis.
3. The Nighantu classification of Jwara into simple (Ekakshata), compounded (Kudakshata), and erratic (Visama) types helps predict prognosis and treatment duration.

# Dravya Guna Vijnana - Properties of Medicinal Substances

Dravya Guna Vijnana is the science of pharmacology in Ayurveda. It describes the properties of medicinal substances based on their Rasa (taste), Guna (qualities), Veerya (potency), Vipaka (post-digestive effect), and Prabhava (special action).

The five tastes (Rasa) are Madhura (sweet), Amla (sour), Lavana (salty), Katu (pungent), Tikta (bitter), and Kashaya (astringent). Each taste has specific effects on doshas and physiological functions. Sweet taste nourishes body tissues and pacifies Vata and Pitta. Sour taste stimulates appetite and pacifies Vata. Salty taste promotes digestion and liquefies Kapha. Pungent taste enhances metabolism and pacifies Kapha. Bitter taste detoxifies and pacifies Pitta. Astringent taste absorbs excess fluids and pacifies Kapha and Pitta.

1. The ten pairs of Guna (qualities) described in Dravya Guna Vijnana include Guru (heavy) vs Laghu (light), Manda (slow) vs Tikshna (sharp), Sheeta (cold) vs Ushna (hot), Snigdha (oily) vs Ruksha (dry), and Slakshna (smooth) vs Khara (rough). Understanding these qualities is essential for proper drug selection.
2. Veerya (potency) determines the immediate action of a substance on the body. It is classified as Ushna (hot) or Sheeta (cold). Hot potency stimulates metabolism and pacifies Kapha. Cold potency reduces inflammation and pacifies Pitta.
3. Vipaka (post-digestive effect) determines the long-term action of a substance after digestion. It is classified as Madhura (sweet), Amla (sour), or Katu (pungent). Sweet Vipaka nourishes tissues and pacifies Vata. Sour Vipaka promotes digestion. Pungent Vipaka reduces Kapha.

# Bhaishajya Kalpana - Pharmaceutical Preparations

Bhaishajya Kalpana describes the methods of preparing Ayurvedic medicines. The seven forms of medication are Swarasa (fresh juice), Kalka (paste), Kashayam (decoction), Arishtam (fermented preparation), Tailam (medicated oil), Ghritam (medicated ghee), and Churnam (powder).

Each dosage form has specific advantages and indications. Swarasa (fresh juice) is most potent for acute conditions. Kashayam (decoction) is ideal for chronic conditions. Tailam (oil) is used for external applications and Basti (enema). Ghritam (ghee) is used for Pitta disorders and as a bioavailability enhancer. Churnam (powder) is convenient for long-term use.

1. Kashayam (decoction) preparation involves boiling coarsely powdered herbs in water until one-fourth or one-eighth of the original volume remains. The standard ratio is 20 grams of herbs per 160 ml of water, reduced to 40 ml. This concentration ensures optimal extraction of water-soluble active compounds.
2. Tailam (medicated oil) preparation involves two stages: Kalka Prakalpana (paste preparation) and Sneha Paka (oil cooking). The standard ratio involves 1 part herb paste, 4 parts base oil, and 16 parts herbal decoction. The mixture is cooked over low heat until all water evaporates.
3. Arishtam (fermented preparation) involves mixing herbal decoction with sugar or jaggery and a fermenting agent. The standard ratio is 1 part herb, 4 parts water for decoction, and equal parts sugar or jaggery. The preparation ferments for 1-3 months in a dark place.
4. Churnam (powder) is prepared by grinding dried herbs to a fine sieve and mixing in specified proportions. The powder should be stored in airtight containers away from moisture and light. Most Churnams have a shelf life of 1-2 years when stored properly.
5. Bhasma (incinerated preparation) involves a rigorous process of purification (Shodhana), calcination (Marana), and testing (Pariksha). The process involves repeated heating and cooling cycles with specific herbal juices. Bhasmas are considered highly potent and bioavailable forms of minerals.

# Yoga Vijnana - Formulation Science

Yoga Vijnana is the science of formulating Ayurvedic medicines. It describes the principles of combining herbs to enhance efficacy, reduce side effects, and create synergistic effects. The seven types of formulations are: Jihvalakshana (based on taste), Karanalakshana (based on action), Kalalakshana (based on time), Dashalakshana (based on ten attributes), Abhilashalakshana (based on desire), Kashayalakshana (based on decoction), and Samskaralakshana (based on processing).

1. Yogavahi (bioavailability enhancers) are substances that enhance the absorption and efficacy of other herbs. Pippali (long pepper), Shunthi (dried ginger), and Maricha (black pepper) are the primary Yogavahi herbs. Trikatu Churna is a classic example of a bioavailability-enhancing formulation.
2. Samana Dravya (equalizing substances) are herbs that balance the properties of a formulation. They help in harmonizing the effects of multiple herbs and reducing potential side effects.
3. Prakriti Dravya (base substances) form the bulk of a formulation and provide the carrier for active ingredients. They include honey, ghee, oil, and water as primary vehicles.
4. The concept of Anupana (vehicle/adjuvant) is crucial in Ayurvedic pharmaceutics. The choice of Anupana can enhance the therapeutic effect of a medicine. Honey is used for Kapha conditions, ghee for Pitta conditions, and warm water for Vata conditions.
5. Viruddha Ahara (incompatible substances) describes substances that should not be combined as they produce toxic effects. Examples include milk with sour fruits, honey with hot water, and fish with milk. Understanding these incompatibilities is essential for safe formulation.

# Nighantu Specific Herb Properties

This section provides detailed properties of commonly used herbs in Ayurvedic practice, organized by their primary therapeutic applications.

1. Tulsi (Ocimum sanctum) is pungent and bitter in taste, with a hot potency and pungent post-digestive effect. It is useful in Kapha disorders, fever, cough, and respiratory infections. Tulsi has adaptogenic properties and helps in managing stress. Fresh leaves are most potent. Dose: 5-10 leaves or 1-3 grams of powder.
2. Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri) is bitter and astringent in taste, with a cold potency and sweet post-digestive effect. It is a powerful nervine tonic that improves memory, cognition, and reduces anxiety. It is useful in children for improving learning capacity. Dose: 1-3 grams of powder with warm water or milk.
3. Shankhpushpi (Convolvulus pluricaulis) is sweet, bitter, and astringent in taste, with a cold potency. It is used as a brain tonic, for anxiety, insomnia, and cognitive decline. It helps in managing stress and promoting mental clarity. Dose: 1-3 grams of powder with warm milk.
4. Mandukaparni (Centella asiatica/gotu kola) is bitter and sweet in taste, with a cold potency. It is useful for wound healing, skin diseases, and as a brain tonic. It enhances collagen synthesis and promotes tissue repair. Dose: 1-3 grams of powder or 20-40 ml of juice.
5. Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus) is sweet and bitter in taste, with a cold potency and sweet post-digestive effect. It is a key herb for women's health, supporting reproductive function and lactation. It also has adaptogenic and immunomodulatory properties. Dose: 3-6 grams of powder with warm milk.

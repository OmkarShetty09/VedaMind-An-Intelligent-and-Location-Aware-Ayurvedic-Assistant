# Sutra Sthana - Introduction to Surgery and Anatomy

The Sushruta Samhita is the foundational text of surgery (Shalya Tantra) in Ayurveda. Sushruta, regarded as the father of surgery, described over 300 surgical procedures and 120 surgical instruments. This section covers the fundamental principles of surgical practice, anatomy, and the classification of surgical conditions.

Sushruta emphasized that a surgeon must possess thorough knowledge of anatomy, proficiency in instrument handling, and surgical dexterity. The text describes the human body in remarkable detail, including descriptions of the skeletal system, musculature, vasculature, and visceral organs.

1. The practice of surgery requires mastery of three fundamental skills: knowledge of anatomy (Sharira Vidya), familiarity with instruments (Yantra Vidya), and manual dexterity (Karma Vidya).
2. Before undertaking any surgical procedure, the physician must assess the patient's constitutional type (Prakriti), current disease state (Vikriti), strength (Bala), and digestive capacity (Agni).
3. The surgeon should be well-versed in the properties of drugs used for anesthesia, wound healing, and post-operative care.

# Sharir Sthana - Human Anatomy

Sushruta's anatomical descriptions are remarkably detailed for his era. He described 300 bones including teeth, 210 joints, 500 muscles, 22 tendons, and 42 tendons associated with vital points (Marma). The Marma points are vital areas of the body where injury can be fatal.

There are 107 Marma points classified by their composition: 41 are of Asthi (bone), 27 are of Sandhi (joint), 20 are of Snayu (ligament/tendon), 16 are of Rakta (blood vessels), and 3 are of Koshta (hollow viscera). Knowledge of these vital points is essential to avoid surgical injury.

1. The Marma points are classified into three categories based on prognosis: Kashtha Marma (mortal, 19 points), Sadhya Marma (dangerous, 33 points), and Rasya Marma (non-dangerous, 55 points).
2. The Sira (blood vessels) are described as channels carrying Rakta (blood) throughout the body. Sushruta identified 24 major Sira that supply blood to different regions.
3. The Dhamani (arteries) carry Prana (vital breath) and are described as pulsating channels. There are 24 Dhamani that distribute vital essence throughout the body.

# Kalpa Sthana - Pharmaceutical Preparations

This section describes the preparation of surgical instruments, cauterization tools, and pharmaceutical formulations used in surgical practice. Sushruta classified surgical instruments into four categories: Yantra (blunt instruments), Sastra (sharp instruments), Upadha (ers) or probes, and Kshara (caustic alkalis).

The text also describes the preparation of Kshara Sutra (medicated caustic thread), which is used in the treatment of fistula-in-anal and hemorrhoids. This technique remains in clinical use today as a standard treatment for anal fistula.

1. Kshara Sutra is prepared by coating a cotton thread with Apamarga Kshara (alkali from Achyranthes aspera), Snuhi Kshara (alkali from Euphorbia neriifolia), and Haridra (turmeric, Curcuma longa). The thread is dried over seven coats.
2. Snuhi Kshara is prepared by collecting the milky latex of Snuhi (Euphorbia neriifolia), mixing it with Haridra powder, and processing it with butter and sesame oil.
3. Agni Karma (thermal cauterization) is performed using instruments made of iron, gold, silver, or copper, each suited to specific conditions. Iron instruments are used for deepest cauterization.

# Chikitsa Sthana - Surgical Treatments

The treatment section of Sushruta Samhita covers the management of various surgical conditions including abscesses, fistula, hemorrhoids, kidney stones, cataracts, and procedures for foreign body removal. The text provides detailed step-by-step surgical techniques.

For the treatment of Bhagandara (fistula-in-anal), Sushruta recommended Kshara Sutra as the primary treatment. The procedure involves identifying the fistula tract, passing the medicated thread through it, and allowing it to gradually cut through the abnormal passage while promoting healing of healthy tissue.

1. Ashthila (hemorrhoids) is classified into four types based on the dominant dosha: Vataja (dry, hard, blue-black), Pittaja (burning, yellow-colored, soft), Kaphaja (slimy, large, pale), and Sannipataja (mixed, severe). Treatment includes Kshara application, Kshara Sutra, and surgical excision for advanced cases.
2. Mutrashmari (kidney and bladder stones) treatment begins with internal medication using Varuna (Crataeva nurvala), Ashmari Bhedana drugs, and lithotriptic formulations. Surgical removal is reserved for stones that do not respond to medical treatment.
3. The treatment of Naktandya (cataract) involves SuTarana (couching procedure) where the lens is displaced downward using a special needle, restoring vision. This is one of the earliest described cataract surgeries.

# Uttara Tantra - Treatments of Eye, Ear, Nose, and Throat

The Uttara Tantra extends Sushruta's surgical expertise to ENT conditions. The text describes procedures for treating diseases of the eye, ear, nose, and throat, including tonsillectomy, foreign body removal, and treatments for nasal conditions.

Sushruta described the anatomy of the ear in detail and recommended surgical intervention for conditions like deafness caused by accumulated cerumen or foreign bodies. The text also describes rhinoplasty (Nasikashastra), which is considered one of the earliest documented plastic surgery procedures.

1. The rhinoplasty procedure (Nasikashastra) described by Sushruta involves using a forehead flap (fasciocutaneous flap) to reconstruct the nose. A leaf-shaped template is cut from a leaf, placed on the forehead, and the flap is raised and rotated to cover the nasal defect.
2. The treatment of Karna Shool (earache) includes instillation of medicated oils such as Bilva Taila, Ashwagandha Taila, and Bhringaraja Taila. If the condition is due to a foreign body, careful removal under direct visualization is recommended.
3. For diseases of the throat including Tundikeri (tonsillitis), Sushruta recommended Guggulu preparations, Kshara application, and in severe cases, surgical removal of the enlarged tonsils using a sharp instrument.

# Karma Sthana - Therapeutic Procedures

This section consolidates the various therapeutic procedures described throughout the text, including bloodletting (Raktamokshana), puncturing (Vedhana), excision (Chedana), scratching (Lekhana), and probing (Vedhana). These Panchakarma procedures form the backbone of Ayurvedic surgical practice.

Raktamokshana (bloodletting) is recommended for conditions caused by blood impurity (Raktapitta), skin diseases (Kushtha), localized pain (Shula), and inflammatory conditions. Sushruta preferred leeches (Jalaukavacharana) for bloodletting as they remove impure blood selectively and secrete hirudin which prevents clotting.

1. Jalaukavacharana (leech therapy) is performed by selecting healthy, active leeches and placing them on the affected area. After the leech has sucked adequate blood, it is removed by applying turmeric powder. The wound is then dressed with antiseptic herbs.
2. Siravyadha (venepuncture) involves puncturing a specific vein to drain impure blood. The technique requires knowledge of venous anatomy and proper instrument handling to avoid damage to adjacent structures.
3. Pracchana (scarification) is performed using a special instrument to make multiple superficial incisions over the affected area for conditions like localized inflammation, abscess formation, and skin diseases.
